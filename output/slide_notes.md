# Slide talking points

1. **Problem:** ZEN studies model recovery/attribution; NoiFi studies adversarial CSI detection. Their metrics serve different tasks.
2. **Implemented:** Installed Python 3.10 and ran compact NoiFi NSM/NRM/NDM modules on 1103 real RoboFiSense recordings. Clean test accuracy: 91.4%.
3. **Compared:** Reconstruction MSE vs NoiFi MED under FGSM and PGD, with a separate calibration split. Use figures/noifi_comparison.png and results/noifi-basic/comparison.csv.
4. **Extended NoiFi:** N=1,3,5,10,20 with three permutation seeds. Compare detection quality, CPU batch timing and score variability in figures/noifi_n_study.png.
5. **Executed ZEN stage:** Upstream attribution on supplied recovered CMRs identified 13/13 base families. Report incomplete upstream artifact coverage.
6. **Extended ZEN:** Used top-two attribution margin to expose ambiguous cases. Smallest margin: mobilenetv2-former (0.0498).
7. **Demonstrated patching:** On 64 controlled local inputs, output error changed from 0.613492 to 0.0 after restoring custom forward bytecode. This is a mechanism demo.
8. **Limitations:** Compact 64x64 architecture, one model-training seed, digital white-box attacks and mixed-location recording split. Full ZEN RAM recovery and official YOLO patch inference were not run.

## Measured comparison to say aloud

- Reconstruction MSE versus NoiFi MED (N=5): FGSM AUROC 0.955 versus 0.926; PGD 0.958 versus 0.968. MED does not improve both attacks in this reduced run.
- N=1 to N=20 barely changes AUROC, reduces score variability, and raises CPU batch time from about 0.48 to 5.22 ms/sample.
- ZEN MR-only attribution identifies 11/13 families, versus 13/13 for combined MR+PR. The missing artifact coverage limits the claim.
